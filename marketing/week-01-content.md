## Post 1 — Educational / tech tip (builds authority)

**Why most startups pick the wrong tech stack (and how to avoid it)**

Most founders choose a tech stack because a friend used it, or a tutorial made it look easy. Neither is a good reason.

The real question: what does your product actually need to do in year 1, and what will it need to do in year 3?

A few things we always check before writing a single line of code:
→ How many users/transactions realistically in the first 12 months?
→ Does the team need to move fast and iterate, or is this a long-lived system that needs to be rock solid?
→ What's your budget for hosting and maintenance, not just the build?
→ Do you need native mobile apps, or does a fast web app cover it for now?

Get these answers right, and the tech stack picks itself. Get them wrong, and you're rebuilding in 18 months.

## Long-form piece (LinkedIn Article — doubles as your first "blog" post until the site has a dedicated blog page)

### 5 Signs Your Business Needs Custom Software (Not Just Another SaaS Subscription)

If you've been signing up for a new SaaS tool every few months just to patch a gap in your workflow, this is for you.

**1. You're paying for software you barely use.**
Most off-the-shelf platforms are built for the "average" business in their category. If you're only using 20% of the features but paying for 100%, that's dead weight in your budget every single month.

**2. Your team has built a "shadow process" around the software.**
When the tool doesn't fit, people improvise — spreadsheets to fill gaps, WhatsApp groups to track what the CRM can't, manual double-entry between two systems. If your real workflow lives in someone's head and a dozen browser tabs, the software has already failed you.

**3. You've outgrown the free/starter tier, but the next tier still doesn't fit.**
This is common with fast-growing businesses. You started on a small plan, hit its limits, upgraded — and the "enterprise" tier is either wildly overpriced or still missing the one feature you actually need.

**4. Integration is duct tape, not a real connection.**
If "integration" means someone manually exporting a CSV from one tool and uploading it to another every week, you don't have an integration — you have a chore. And chores don't scale.

**5. Your competitive edge lives in a process, not a product.**
If the way you run operations, price jobs, or manage inventory is actually *why* your customers choose you over competitors — that process deserves purpose-built software, not a generic template shared with a thousand other companies including your competitors.

**What custom software actually costs vs. what people assume**
The biggest myth: custom software is only for large enterprises with big budgets. In reality, a well-scoped custom tool — built around exactly what you need and nothing else — often costs less over 2-3 years than stacking multiple SaaS subscriptions and the hidden labor cost of working around their limits.
